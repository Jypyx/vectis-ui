# Popover

Generic floating panel, to show any content anchored to a trigger. It rests on the native
**Popover API** (top layer, light dismiss) and on **CSS Anchor Positioning**: no dependency, no
position computation in JavaScript. The component's only JS is the bridge between
`v-model:open` and the imperative popover API.

VPopover carries **the plumbing and nothing else**: the `[popover]` element and its mode, the
open state, the anchoring, the placement and the surface. It imposes **no ARIA role, no
keyboard shortcut and no focus management** — that is precisely what distinguishes a menu from
a listbox or a date picker, and it stays the responsibility of the component using it. That is
how it serves as the brick under the design system's panels (VCombobox, VTooltip, VDateInput,
VTimePicker), each keeping its own semantics and keyboard.

## Trigger

The trigger is supplied through the scoped `#trigger` slot: setting `v-bind="triggerProps"` on
the element attaches `popovertarget`, `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls` — the _disclosure_
pattern. It is the native `popovertarget` attribute that opens and closes the panel: no click
handler is needed, and the invoked element becomes the panel's **implicit anchor**.

The wrapper around the trigger carries the `anchor-name`, confined to its subtree by
`anchor-scope`. That confinement is what allows a **static, shared** anchor name across every
instance on the page: without it, a shown panel — in the top layer, and therefore considered
"after" the whole document — would attach to the last named wrapper on the page.

## Opening and closing

`v-model:open` reflects the panel's real state in both directions: the model opens and closes,
and any close coming from the browser (light dismiss, Escape) sets it back to `false`. The state
is never set by hand inside the component; it is always read from the panel's
`beforetoggle`/`toggle` events — which is what guarantees it never drifts.

Two modes:

- **`auto`** (default) — native light dismiss: a click outside or Escape closes the panel, and
  open popovers form a stack (opening the second closes the first).
- **`manual`** — nothing is automatic. Reserve it for cases where the consuming component drives
  the dismissal itself (focus loss, selection, confirmation), as VTooltip and the date and time
  pickers do.

The trigger needs no click handler: `popovertarget` toggles the panel natively, and
`aria-expanded` follows the real state.

## Placements

Twelve placements through `data-placement` → `position-area` (the shared `floating.css` sheet,
common to VMenu and VTooltip). Aligned placements (`top-start`, `right-end`…) cancel the margin
on the aligned side so they stay flush with the trigger's edge.

## Surface

By default the panel carries the design system's common surface (background, border, shadow,
radius). `surface: false` removes it entirely: the content then supplies its own decoration.
This is the mode used by the DS panels that have their own chrome — VTooltip (inverted contrast)
and the date and time pickers.

## Interactive content

Unlike VTooltip, the panel is persistent and focus can enter it: forms, links and buttons are
usable inside. Since the component imposes no role, dialog-like content must be declared
explicitly by the consumer.

## Anchor supplied by the consumer

When the component using VPopover already owns a root and a control — a text input, for
instance, on which `popovertarget` would be invalid — it sets `anchor-scope` on its root and
`anchor-name` on its control itself, then passes the name through the `anchor` prop. VPopover
then renders **no layout node**: the `#trigger` slot is omitted and its wrapper switches to
`display: contents`.

## Accessibility

- **No default role.** The panel is a neutral container: for dialog-like content, set
  `role="dialog"` and an accessible name (`aria-label` or `aria-labelledby`) on the VPopover, and
  `aria-haspopup="dialog"` on the trigger.
- `triggerProps` supplies `aria-expanded` and `aria-controls`: the panel's state is announced on
  the trigger, which stays the focusable element.
- **Focus is not moved** on opening and is not trapped in the panel: this is a non-modal panel.
  Content that must capture focus belongs to the VDialog component.
- In `auto` mode, Escape closes the panel and the browser returns focus to the trigger. In
  `manual` mode, both behaviours belong to the consumer.
- The appearance animation (shared with `.v-floating`) respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.
