# HoverCard

A card previewing what a link leads to, such as a profile behind a mention, shown when the
pointer or the keyboard focus rests on the trigger. It is a `popover="manual"` driven by the
component: no stable HTML primitive opens a panel on hover with a delay, and manual mode leaves
an open menu or popover untouched. The card is anchored in CSS, like VTooltip and VPopover.

The trigger comes through the scoped default slot: binding `triggerProps` on it sets
`aria-details`. The card's content goes in the `#content` slot and may hold links and buttons.

## Opening and closing

The card opens once the pointer has rested on the trigger for `openDelay` (500 ms), and closes
`closeDelay` (300 ms) after the pointer has left both the trigger and the card. That second
delay is what lets the pointer cross the gap and stay on the card to use it. Pressing the
trigger closes the card, so it never stands over what the trigger opens.

<Canvas of={VHoverCardStories.OpenOnHover} />

## Keyboard

Keyboard focus on the trigger opens the card after the same delay. The card follows the trigger
in the DOM, so Tab moves into its content; it closes once the focus has left both. Escape closes
it from anywhere on the page and hands a focus inside the card back to the trigger. A focus
given by a click does not open it.

<Canvas of={VHoverCardStories.KeyboardAccess} />

## In running text

The wrapper is inline, so a trigger sits in a sentence. The card is a `<span>` and its content
mounts on first opening: a trigger inside a `<p>` still produces valid server-rendered HTML.

<Canvas of={VHoverCardStories.InText} />

## Loading on open

`v-model:open` reports every opening, hover included, which is the moment to fetch what the card
shows. Setting it opens or closes the card at once, without the delays.

<Canvas of={VHoverCardStories.LoadOnOpen} />

## Exposed methods

`show()` opens the card at once, `close()` closes it, and `el` is the card element.

## Accessibility

- `aria-details` tells assistive technology the trigger has details, without reading the whole
  card on every focus as `aria-describedby` would. Support varies between screen readers; the
  DOM order still places the card right after the trigger.
- **A tap opens nothing.** There is no hover on a touch screen, so the trigger keeps its usual
  meaning: whatever the card shows must also be reachable at the trigger's destination.
- Escape dismisses the card without moving the pointer (WCAG 1.4.13), and the key is spent on it:
  inside a VDialog it does not close the dialog as well.
- The card stays while hovered or focused, so its content can be read and used.
- The appearance animation, shared with `.v-floating`, respects `prefers-reduced-motion`.
