# SkeletonLoader

A **100% CSS** loading placeholder: the silhouette of a design system component while its content
is on its way. Two animations to choose from — a band of brightness crossing it (`wave`) or a
highlight rising and falling (`pulse`) — plus `none` to freeze it.

The principle is **a declared silhouette, never a measured one**: the component does not read the
DOM and deduces nothing from the content it replaces. You say which shape to take, and the
component builds it from the design system tokens — the control scale for the heights, the
inherited typography for the lines of text. No behavioural JavaScript, no browser access, hence
nothing to hydrate on the server side.

## Shapes

Each `shape` value answers a question CSS alone cannot guess: which radius, and where the height
comes from.

| `shape`   | Targets                      | Radius      | Height                           |
| --------- | ---------------------------- | ----------- | -------------------------------- |
| `text`    | a line of text, a paragraph  | pill        | the inherited typography (`1em`) |
| `control` | VButton, VInput, a select    | interactive | the control scale (`size`)       |
| `pill`    | VChip, VBadge                | pill        | the control scale                |
| `circle`  | VAvatar, a round icon button | pill + 1:1  | the control scale                |
| `surface` | a card, an image, a block    | surface     | a dedicated token                |

<Canvas of={VSkeletonLoaderStories.Shapes} />

The circle transfers its width from its height: `size` alone is enough to change its diameter.

## Replacing a design system component

The recipe is two gestures: pick the shape matching the component's role, and give it the same
size. Since the heights come from the same table as the controls', an `md` skeleton is exactly the
height of an `md` VButton — the page does not move on replacement.

<Canvas of={VSkeletonLoaderStories.DesignSystemSilhouettes} />

For a component made of several parts — a field with its label, a card with its title and its text
— stack several skeletons rather than looking for one that would do everything: composition is
what reproduces the structure.

## Sizes

`size` and `compact` take the design system's control scale. They only act on `control`, `pill` and
`circle`: a line of text follows its parent's typography, and a surface has a height of its own.

<Canvas of={VSkeletonLoaderStories.Sizes} />

## Sizing

`width` and `height` accept a number — read as pixels — or any CSS length. They win over `shape`
and over `size`.

Neither is mandatory: with no dimension, the silhouette takes the available width and the height of
its shape. A consumer's style also stays authoritative throughout — the design system CSS lives in
a cascade layer, which any rule outside it wins over without having to insist. So a skeleton is
sized just as well by its parent grid as by its props.

## Paragraphs

`lines` stacks several silhouettes. In the `text` shape, the last one is shortened: that detail is
what reads as a "block of text" rather than a "table".

<Canvas of={VSkeletonLoaderStories.Paragraph} />

A line's height is one em and the gutter is the remaining leading, so that N lines occupy exactly N
lines of text whatever the parent's typography.

## Animations

<Canvas of={VSkeletonLoaderStories.Animations} />

`wave` is the most demonstrative, and it is the default; its interest starts on large surfaces,
since a band crossing a small circle is no more than a flicker — there, `pulse` stays readable.
`none` serves printing, screenshots, and the case where the animation is already carried by a
parent.

## Colour

<Canvas of={VSkeletonLoaderStories.CustomColor} />

`color` replaces the background. The wave's highlight has no separate setting: it is **derived**
from the background by a lightening, which keeps it right on an arbitrary colour as well as from
one theme to the other. That is also why the component works in a dark theme with no dedicated
rule: aiming at a target colour would invert from one theme to the other, applying a lightness
delta would not.

There is deliberately no semantic `tone`: a skeleton represents unknown content, and tinting it
success or danger would make it say something false.

## Content replacement

<Canvas of={VSkeletonLoaderStories.ProgressiveReplacement} />

There is no wrapper mode taking the content in a slot. That would open the door to measuring the
DOM, and it would not spare you declaring the shape anyway: the idiom stays a `v-if`/`v-else`, with
`aria-busy` on the zone that will receive the content.

## Accessibility

- **Decorative by default.** The component is hidden from assistive technologies: a loading page
  commonly holds a dozen silhouettes, and as many competing announcements would make the wait
  unreadable.
- **The announcement belongs to the container, not to the placeholder.** Set `aria-busy` on the
  zone that will receive the content — the same split as in `VButton`, where the button carries the
  state and the spinner is only a drawing.
- `announce` (and `label`, which implies it) is the escape hatch: the silhouette becomes a status
  region with a visually hidden label. Reserve it for **one** instance per zone, and preferably
  with a situated label — "Loading the results…" rather than the generic default.
  <Canvas of={VSkeletonLoaderStories.Announcement} />
- Do not combine `announce` and `aria-busy` on the same subtree: the information would be announced
  twice.
- No information is carried by colour or by contrast: there is no text to read in a silhouette.
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: the `wave` falls back to the pulse, heavily slowed. A translation is
  precisely what these users are flagging, but a motionless placeholder would no longer say "in
  progress" — the animation is therefore transformed, never removed.

This component's accessible labels come from the design system dictionary: they translate globally
(see **Foundations › Internationalization**), and the text props stay authoritative for a
case-by-case adjustment.
