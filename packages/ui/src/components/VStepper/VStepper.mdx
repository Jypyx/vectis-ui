# Stepper

The steps of a process and where the reader stands in it. VStepper is navigation only: the content
of each step is yours, rendered from the same `v-model`, along with your own Back and Next buttons.

## Steps and model

`steps` lists the steps in order: a `value`, a `title`, and optionally a `description`, `error`,
`completed` and `disabled`. `v-model` holds the value of the current step; left out or unknown,
the first step is current.

Each step is drawn in one of four states. `error` wins over everything. Otherwise the current step
is `active`, the steps before it are `completed` and those after it `upcoming`, unless `completed`
says otherwise for a step.

<Canvas of={VStepperStories.States} />

## Going back and forth

By default, the steps already reached are buttons and the steps ahead are plain text: the reader
goes back freely, and forward only as far as they have been. Going back to the first step leaves
the steps reached since within reach. `nonLinear` makes every step a button. A `disabled` step is
never one.

## Narrow space

A horizontal stepper is a size container. Below 36rem, only the current step keeps its title on
screen; the others keep their circles, and their titles stay readable by assistive technology.
As a container, it takes its width from its parent rather than from its steps: inside a flex
row, give it a basis of its own.

<Canvas of={VStepperStories.Narrow} />

## Accessibility

- The steps are an `<ol>`, named "Progress" from the dictionary (`label`), so their number and
  position are announced.
- The current step carries `aria-current="step"`.
- The tick and the exclamation mark are hidden from assistive technology; "Completed" and "Error"
  are said after the title instead.
- Steps within reach are native buttons in the tab order; the others are not focusable.
- In forced colours, the current step keeps a ring of its own and the connectors stay drawn.
