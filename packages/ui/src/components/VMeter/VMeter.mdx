# Meter

A measurement within a known range: disk usage, a battery level, the strength of a password. Unlike
VProgressLinear, the value is not on its way to completion; it is a reading, which may be good or
bad depending on where it sits.

The bar carries the `meter` role with `aria-valuenow`, `aria-valuemin`, `aria-valuemax` and
`aria-valuetext`, rather than using the native `<meter>`: that element is styled only through vendor
pseudo-elements, one rule set per engine, does not animate its fill in Firefox and cannot be split
into segments. The semantics announced to screen readers are the same.

## Regions

`low`, `high` and `optimum` follow the rules of the native `<meter>`. They divide the range into a
low part, a middle part and a high part; the part holding `optimum` is good, the part next to it
average, and the far part poor. The tone follows: `success`, `warning`, then `danger`. Without any
of the three, the bar is in the accent colour.

- Optimum between `low` and `high` (the default, at the middle of the range): the middle is good and
  both ends are average, as for a humidity level.
- Optimum below `low`: low values are good, as for a processor load.
- Optimum above `high`: high values are good, as for a battery.

<Canvas of={VMeterStories.Regions} />

Colour alone does not tell the region apart. When the region matters, say it in words through
`valueText`, as the password strength below does.

## Tones

`tone` fixes the colour, whatever the region. `color` takes a colour of your own, and the track is
derived from it against the theme.

<Canvas of={VMeterStories.Tones} />

## Label and value

`label` is written above the bar and names it by reference: there is no other way for a screen
reader to learn what is measured. Without it, the bar takes a generic name from the dictionary;
an `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby` of your own wins over both.

The value is written at the end of the same line. It is a percentage of the range by default.
`formatOptions` formats the value itself for the current locale with `Intl.NumberFormat`, such as a
unit or a currency, and `valueText` replaces it with your own words. Screen readers read that same
text through `aria-valuetext`, so the copy on screen is hidden from them.

<Canvas of={VMeterStories.ValueText} />

`hideLabel` and `hideValue` remove the text above the bar without removing what screen readers
hear.

## Segments

`segments` splits the bar into equal parts separated by a gap. The fill runs through them in
order, so a value between two steps fills part of a segment.

<Canvas of={VMeterStories.Segments} />

## Changing value

The fill moves to the new value and the tone changes with the region. The movement is removed when
reduced motion is requested. A meter is not a live region: announce a change that matters
elsewhere.

<Canvas of={VMeterStories.Live} />

## Sizes

`size` sets the thickness of the bar, 4, 8 or 12 pixels, and the text above it is smaller at `sm`.
The length is the container's: the meter takes the whole width available.

<Canvas of={VMeterStories.Sizes} />

## Right to left

The bar fills from the reading start, and the segments follow the same direction.

<Canvas of={VMeterStories.RightToLeft} />

## Accessibility

- The `meter` role exposes the value, its bounds and its text. Give every meter a `label`, visible
  or hidden.
- Colour shows the region but never alone: put the meaning into `valueText` when it matters.
- In forced colours, the track is outlined and the fill painted in the system highlight colour.
