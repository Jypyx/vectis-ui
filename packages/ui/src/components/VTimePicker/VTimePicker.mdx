# TimePicker

A clock shown directly in the page, for choosing a time. It is the hour-and-minute counterpart of
[VDatePicker](?path=/docs/components-datepicker--docs): it holds **all** the logic of pointing,
stepping and announcing, while [VTimeInput](?path=/docs/components-timeinput--docs) does no more
than dress it in a text field and a popover.

It is read in three parts — the two large numerals saying what has been chosen, the choice of
half-day beside them, and the face one points at.

## The value

`v-model` is always a **24-hour `"HH:mm"` string**, whatever clock is on display: a consumer never
has to know which one the reader's language uses. With no value at all the clock shows midnight —
deliberately not the current time, since reading the clock while rendering would make a page drawn
on a server disagree with the same page in the browser. A component that wants to open on the
current time sets it from a handler, which is exactly what `VTimeInput` does.

## The two clocks

The `format` prop decides between a 12- and a 24-hour face; left out, the resolved locale decides.
A 24-hour clock carries a **second ring inside the first** for the hours 13 to 00, and has no
half-day control — the numerals then centre on their own.

<Canvas of={VTimePickerStories.TwentyFourHour} />

## AM and PM

On a 12-hour clock the half-day is a control of its own, beside the numerals, because the hand
cannot express it: walking past twelve on a 12-hour face brings you back to one, in the same half of the
day. It writes straight into the value, and it is remembered even while there is nothing to
convert — so choosing PM on an empty clock and then an hour gives the afternoon.

<Canvas of={VTimePickerStories.Meridiem} />

## Actions

The footer is a **slot**: a clock shown on its own has nothing to confirm, while one inside a panel
needs Cancel and OK. `VTimeInput` fills it with exactly those two, and keeps the draft behind them
so that only OK writes the value.

<Canvas of={VTimePickerStories.Footer} />

The component also emits **`confirm`** when the reader settles the minutes from the keyboard —
never when the pointer is merely released, since letting go of the hand is how one stops adjusting
it, not how one confirms.

## Steps and intervals

`minuteStep` is the interval the minutes snap to, under the pointer as on the arrow keys.

## Language

`locale` takes precedence over the design system's global locale, and falls back to it. Words and
formats resolve separately: a language with no dictionary of its own still gets its own hour cycle.

## Accessibility

- The face is a single **`role="slider"`**, and it is the only focusable element in it. The numerals
  around it are `aria-hidden` visual markers: they are reached by the pointer's angle rather than one
  cell at a time, so the whole spoken value has to be carried by that one element — `aria-valuenow`,
  `aria-valuemin`, `aria-valuemax` and a localized `aria-valuetext`.
- Its accessible name follows the step, "Hour" or "Minutes".
- A **polite live region** repeats the step whenever the reader moves it. A change of accessible
  name alone is not reliably announced. Putting the clock back on the hour through `reset()` stays
  silent, because that is not something the reader did.
- The keyboard is a slider's: arrows step, `Home` and `End` go to the ends, `PageUp`/`PageDown` move
  the minutes by five, and `Enter` moves from the hour to the minutes and then confirms.
- The arrows are **not** flipped in a right-to-left page, and neither is the face: clockwise means
  the same thing everywhere. Only the half-day control follows the reading direction.
- The selected numeral is listed in the axe contrast exclusions, and that is a tool limitation
  rather than a real violation: the numeral is painted over the hand's dot, a **sibling** it only
  partly covers, so axe measures it against whatever is underneath instead.

## Exposed methods

- **`focus()`** — moves the focus onto the face, which is what the arrows drive.
- **`reset()`** — puts the clock back on the hour and clears the announcement, without announcing
  anything. A panel around it calls this as it opens and closes, so every visit starts on the hour.
