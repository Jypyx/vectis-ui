# Slider

A slider built on the native `<input type="range">` — keyboard, the ARIA slider role and form
participation all for free. The JavaScript is limited to keeping a range ordered, the
refusal `readonly` asks for, the numeric fields' bridge and deriving the step positions (ticks and
labels); no browser behaviour is reimplemented.

The track (6px, the `--vectis-control-size-slider-track` token), the fill, the ticks, the labels and
the bubbles all align on the **thumb's real centre** — which travels `[thumb/2, 100% − thumb/2]`, not
`[0, 100%]` — through unitless fractions set as custom properties. Clicking the track moves the
value (a jump + an immediate drag, natively); on hover, the thumb mixes a little accent into
its own white rather than reading a surface token, which would turn dark with the theme and
flash a white thumb nearly black.

## Range

`range` superimposes two native inputs (there is no dual-thumb primitive) — only their thumbs
capture the pointer, and the JS keeps the pair ordered: a thumb taken past its sibling pushes
it along, so a range that has closed onto a single value can always be opened again. The v-model becomes a
`[start, end]` tuple and the accessible labels are suffixed "(start)" / "(end)". Clicking the track
is disabled in this mode (it would be ambiguous).

`@input` fires at every step of a drag and `@change` once the reader settles, both from either
thumb and both carrying the whole `[start, end]` pair. They are declared events rather than
attributes left to fall through, which would only ever have reached one of the two inputs.

## Steps and ticks

`step` rests on the native stepping (pointer as well as keyboard). The opt-in `ticks` prop
materializes each step as a dot on the track (4px, derived from the track token), contrasted
depending on whether the fill covers it. A guard: past 50 steps the ticks are not rendered (with a
development warning).

<Canvas of={VSliderStories.Steps} />

## Step labels

`labels` associates a label with each step, displayed under the slider (to its right in vertical): a
string is rendered as text, an object `{ icon, label }` as an icon (a name or an explicit render)
with an accessible label. Supplying the labels enables the ticks implicitly and sets `aria-valuetext`
on the inputs: screen readers announce "M", not "2".

## Numeric fields

`inputs` adds `<VInput type="number">` fields for precise adjustment: one after the slider in single
mode, one per bound in range mode. The entry is validated on `change` (blur or Enter), never while
typing: parse, clamp to the bounds, snap to the last step that fits and push the sibling thumb if
needed; an empty or invalid entry
silently reverts to the current value.

<Canvas of={VSliderStories.WithInputs} />

`size` sets the height of those fields, `md` by default like every other field of the design
system. Inside a VInputGroup the row's size and its `disabled` win, the arbitration every field
follows.

## Tooltip

`tooltip` displays a value bubble following the thumb during the drag and on keyboard focus (only
the bubble of the manipulated thumb appears). It displays the step's label when `labels` is
supplied, the value otherwise. It is positioned by the same fractions as the fill — no anchor
positioning: the native thumb is a pseudo-element and cannot be an anchor.

<Canvas of={VSliderStories.WithTooltip} />

## Vertical

`orientation="vertical"`: min at the bottom, max at the top, through `writing-mode: vertical-lr` +
`direction: rtl` on the track's subcontainer — the native element handles the drag axis, the keyboard
(up arrow = +) and the ARIA orientation. Default length `--vectis-control-size-slider-length`
(10rem), overridable by setting the token on the element. Support: Chrome/Edge 129+, Safari 18.1+ (a
slight raise of the floor, accepted; see CLAUDE.md).

## States

`disabled` switches the track, the fill, the ticks, the labels and the thumb to greys
(`--vectis-color-surface-muted`, `--vectis-color-text-subtle`) — no opacity trick, and the rendering
follows the dark theme automatically:

<Canvas of={VSliderStories.Disabled} />

`readonly` shows the value without letting it change. A range input has no native read-only, so
the component cancels the keys that move a thumb and, for the pointer, puts back a thumb the browser
has already moved before it is painted. The thumbs stay focusable and carry `aria-readonly`, the
number fields turn read-only with them, and the fill and the thumbs' ring take
`--vectis-color-text-muted`.

<Canvas of={VSliderStories.ReadOnly} />

`invalid` rings the thumbs in `--vectis-color-danger`, as VCheckbox and VRadio colour their
border, and sets `aria-invalid` on each thumb and field.

<Canvas of={VSliderStories.Invalid} />

## Forms

The root is a layout box holding the labels, the track and the optional number fields, so the
attributes you put on the component are redirected onto the real `<input type="range">`:
`name`, `id`, `required` and the `aria-*` all land there rather than on the wrapper, where a
`name` would submit nothing and a `<label for>` would point at a div. Only `class` and `style`
stay on the root, which is the box you position.

A **range** has two thumbs and therefore no single value to submit. They go to the end thumb, and
the component says so in development: bind the model to two inputs of your own when a form has to
receive both bounds.

The accessible name goes through `label`, and an `aria-label` or `aria-labelledby` of your own
takes precedence over it. Whichever you give is what names each thumb of a range, which is why
the two are announced as its start and its end rather than as two sliders sharing one name.

`label` **draws nothing**: it is an accessible name, unlike the `label` of VInput and the other
text fields, which is rendered above the field. A visible caption for the slider belongs to the page
around it, as it does for VInputOTP. What the component does draw is `hint`, a line of help under
the track, tied to the thumb through `aria-describedby` and appended to whatever you pointed at
yourself.

## Accessibility

- Always name the slider: `label` does it, and so does an `aria-label` or an `aria-labelledby` of
  your own, which take precedence over the prop. Whichever you use names the range input(s)
  (suffixed "(start)" / "(end)" in range mode) as well as the numeric fields.
- With `labels`, `aria-valuetext` translates the numeric value into a step label; the labels' icons
  carry their accessible label (an `aria-label` through VIcon).
- The tooltip bubbles are `aria-hidden`: the value is already announced by the slider role.
- Focus is visible through a ring (`--vectis-focus-ring-*`) on the thumb; the bubble also appears on
  keyboard focus.
- The transitions (the thumb's background, the bubbles) respect `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- In Windows forced colors the track draws its own edge and the fill takes the system
  `Highlight`. The bar is painted almost entirely in backgrounds, which that mode flattens to
  `Canvas`, so without those rules only the thumb's border would be left on screen.

This component's accessible labels come from the design system dictionary: they translate globally
(see **Foundations › Internationalization**), and the text props stay authoritative for a
case-by-case adjustment.
