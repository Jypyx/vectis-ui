# Carousel

Scrolls through content — images or text — horizontally or vertically. `VCarousel` invents no
scrolling: it is a native `scroll-snap` container, so touch, trackpad, the scrollbar, the keyboard
and the snapping all come from the browser. The slides are declared as `VCarouselItem` children,
and `v-model` carries the index of the current one.

## Slides per view, and the responsive rule

`itemsPerView` is a **maximum**, not a target, and `itemMinSize` is the floor below which a slide
must not shrink. The browser fits as many slides as it can and scrolls further when it cannot —
that arithmetic lives in the slide's `flex-basis`, which is the whole responsiveness: no
breakpoint, no container query, no observer, and therefore nothing to keep in step with a design
token.

## Pages, not slides

`v-model` is a slide index, but not every slide can become the leading one. With
`scroll-snap-align: start`, a position past the end of the track is unreachable — the scroller
clamps short of it. Six slides three at a time therefore offer **four** positions, not six, and the
component renders four indicators and stops `next` on the fourth. Anything else would hand out dots
that scroll nowhere and let the model hold a value the DOM can never satisfy.

That count is measured off the scroller rather than computed from `itemsPerView`, because a `peek`
or an active `itemMinSize` makes the real slides-per-view fractional, and the fractional part costs
one further position. Before there is any layout — on the server, and on the first paint — it falls
back to `count - itemsPerView + 1`, which is exact in the default configuration.

The indicators stay named after the slide they scroll to (`1 of 6` … `4 of 6`): the control parks
that slide at the start edge, so the name agrees with the slide's own label and with the live
region. A page ordinal would announce "4 of 4" on a control that reveals slide 4 of 6.

<Canvas of={VCarouselStories.Pages} />

The count follows a resize with no `ResizeObserver`: the number of positions can only change when
the number of fully visible slides changes, which crosses the intersection observer's `1.0`
threshold and re-triggers the measurement.

## Peek

`peek` reserves a strip past the last fully visible slide so the next one shows through — the
product-list template. It **includes the gap that precedes it**, which is what lets one formula
serve every combination without a special case.

One consequence to design around: with a peek, the last slide is never fully revealed. The last
snap position stops short of the end of the track by the width of the strip, and that is inherent
to `scroll-snap-align: start` under mandatory snapping.

## Effects

`effect` picks the transition, and all four are **CSS scroll-driven animations**: they are keyed
to the slide's position in the scrollport, so they play under the finger during a drag instead of
running a fixed-duration transition.

- `slide` — no animation beyond the scrolling itself. Works with any `itemsPerView` and any `peek`.
- `fade` — a dissolve in place. It counter-translates the slide to cancel the scroll travel, which
  only works when a slide is exactly the size of the viewport: with several items per view, or with
  a `peek`, every slide would pile onto the same spot, so the effect **downgrades to `slide`** and
  says so in the console.
- `scale` — the slide grows into place as it reaches the centre. Fine at any `itemsPerView`.
- `cover` — a 3D coverflow: the neighbours tilt away and sink back.

Where scroll-driven animations are unavailable, nothing is declared and the carousel is a plain
`slide` — the same progressive-enhancement contract as anchor positioning elsewhere in the DS.
`prefers-reduced-motion` cancels them everywhere, and stops autoplay outright.

<Canvas of={VCarouselStories.Effects} />

Two invariants worth knowing before touching the sheet: the animation is carried by an inner box
and never by the slide itself, because a scroll-snap area is the element's _transformed_ border
box — animating the slide would make its snap position depend on the scroll position, and the
symptom is jitter rather than an error. And the animation properties are written as longhands,
never as the `animation` shorthand, which would silently reset the timeline and the duration.

## Orientation

`orientation="vertical"` scrolls on the block axis. Give it a `height`: a percentage `flex-basis`
has no definite reference on the block axis, and without one every slide would collapse onto its
content.

Both affordances follow the axis: `outside` controls go above and below, and the indicators move to
the inline end. Note the asymmetry between the orientations — horizontal `outside` controls cost
inline size at a constant footprint, where vertical ones cost block footprint at a constant slide
size, because the block axis is sized by `height` rather than by the container.

## Controls and indicators

`controls` and `indicators` each take `false`, `'inside'` (over the slides) or `'outside'`.

The two are placed independently, and the previous/next pair is always centred on **the slides
alone** — never on the slides plus the indicators. That is why the indicator bar is not a child of
the box the controls are positioned against: inside it, the bar's height would drag the pair below
the middle of the slides.

Placement per orientation:

|                         | horizontal                           | vertical                            |
| ----------------------- | ------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------- |
| `controls: 'inside'`    | over the slides, at the inline edges | over the slides, at the block edges |
| `controls: 'outside'`   | left and right of the slides         | above and below the slides          |
| `indicators: 'inside'`  | over the slides, bottom-centre       | over the slides, at the inline end  |
| `indicators: 'outside'` | below the slides                     | to the inline end of the slides     |

`controls="outside"` reserves its room as **padding on the component**, so the footprint never
changes and the slides narrow instead — a carousel does not suddenly overflow its neighbours when
you move its controls out. The reserved width tracks the size the buttons actually render at.

`indicators="inside"` draws no surface behind the dots: a pill would cover a slice of the slide the
whole time, which is a lot of media to spend on a few 8px marks. The dots carry their own
legibility instead — a white fill with a dark hairline, the pair that reads on light and dark media
alike — and they are deliberately theme-independent, since their backdrop is your content rather
than the page. Over media busy enough to swallow them anyway, use `indicators="outside"` or the
`#indicator` slot.

Icons and labels are props (`prevIcon`, `nextIcon`, `prevLabel`…), and the `#controls`,
`#indicators` and `#indicator` slots replace the rendering entirely — `#indicators`
receives `goTo`, without which the bar could no longer select anything, and both bars receive
`orientation`, without which a custom one cannot pick its icons or its axis.

A custom bar must render one control per **`pageCount`**, not per `count`: the slots receive both,
`count` being there for "N of M" labels. Three consequences of replacing a bar, since the slot
takes over the container the DS's placement CSS is keyed on: a custom `#controls` lays itself out
inside the stage rather than being positioned for you, `controls="outside"` then reserves no
gutter (its footprint is yours to decide), and `controlsVisibility` is inert on it. A custom
`#indicators` is a direct child of the component, so in the vertical orientation it sits **beside**
the slides rather than below them.

## Right-to-left

The layout mirrors itself: everything is written in logical properties, so the scroll axis, the
snapping, the slide sizing and the placement of both bars follow the direction with no branch —
and `space-between` puts `previous` at the inline start, which is the right-hand side. The glyphs
are the exception, because a chevron points at a physical direction: the control icons are flipped
in CSS, so `previous` points right and `next` points left.

## Revealing the controls

`controlsVisibility` takes `'always'` (the default) or `'hover'`, which fades the previous/next
pair in on hover — or on **keyboard** focus anywhere in the carousel, which is what keeps it usable
without a pointer. The reveal boundary is deliberately the same one autoplay pauses on: one
boundary, two behaviours, nothing to keep in step.

Keyboard focus, and not any focus, because a pointer click leaves the focus on the control it hit.
Reading it as "focused" would keep the pair revealed — and autoplay paused — until you clicked
somewhere outside the carousel, long after the pointer had left. `:focus-visible` is the
distinction the platform already draws: a click does not match it, a Tab does.

The pair is **revealed, never removed**. `display: none` and `visibility: hidden` would drop the
buttons from the tab order and from the accessibility tree; an opacity leaves both intact, and
costs no layout, so nothing shifts as it fades. On a device with no hover to give — a touch screen
— the mode is inert and the pair stays permanently visible, and the indicators are never hidden by
this prop at all, so a navigation affordance always exists.

## Autoplay

`autoplay` is an interval in milliseconds; `0` disables it. It stops on the last page — there is no
loop, which would mean cloning slides and repositioning the scroller in JS. It pauses on hover and
on keyboard focus, and `prefers-reduced-motion` disables it completely: a media query stops a
transition, never a timer.

The component renders **no pause button**. The prop is reactive and `0` cancels the timer
immediately, so a stop control is a binding on your side:

```vue
<VCarousel :autoplay="paused ? 0 : 5000">…</VCarousel>
```

One is worth adding. WCAG 2.2.2 asks for a way to stop content that starts moving on its own and
runs for more than five seconds, and hover and focus leave a touch user with neither — the
exemption only holds while `interval × (pages − 1)` stays under five seconds.

## Accessibility

- The root is a `role="region"` with a translated `aria-roledescription`, named by `label`; a
  consumer `aria-label` wins over it and an `aria-labelledby` replaces both. **Give every carousel
  on a page a distinct name**: a region is a landmark, and two identically named landmarks are a
  real violation.
- Each slide is a `role="group"` with `aria-roledescription="slide"` and a "N of M" name. The
  count comes from the slot's VNodes, so it is identical on the server and on the client — a
  registry the slides fed at mount would render zero of them server-side.
- The scroll track carries `tabindex="0"`, and that is required rather than defensive: a scroll
  container must have a tabbable descendant, and slide content may hold nothing focusable. It is
  also what makes the track the keyboard target: the arrows move one slide and Home/End jump to
  the ends. A focused scroll container does move on the arrows by itself, but the browser scrolls
  it a fixed pixel step that mandatory snapping immediately undoes, so the component handles them.
- The indicators are ordinary buttons named "N of M", the current one marked with `aria-current`.
  Their hit area is 24px even though the dot is 8px.
- Autoplay ships no control of its own, so if you enable it, add a stop control by binding
  `autoplay` to `0` — see above.
- A visually hidden `role="status"` announces the position — and stays silent while autoplay
  rotates, because narrating an auto-rotating carousel floods the screen reader.
- `controlsVisibility="hover"` hides the pair with an opacity, so the buttons keep their place in
  the tab order and in the accessibility tree; keyboard focus anywhere in the carousel brings them
  back, and
  a pointer that cannot hover never hides them in the first place.
