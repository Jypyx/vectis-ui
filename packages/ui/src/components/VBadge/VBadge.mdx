# Badge

A **non-interactive** information pill: a counter or an icon. No hover, no focus, no event — the
information alone. Two formats (a 20px pill or a 10px dot through `dot`), three placements
(standalone, inline, `overlay`), and a single full-colour rendering driven by `tone` or a custom
colour. For a **textual** status, use a `VChip`.

## Tones

Five tones (`neutral`, `accent` — the default —, `success`, `warning`, `danger`) in full-colour
rendering. The text colour adapts **black/white** through `contrast-color()` (Safari 26+);
browsers without support fall back to a per-tone value (`--vectis-color-text-on-accent`,
`--vectis-color-text-on-warning`…) — the library's classic solid rendering.

The particularity of the `neutral` tone: a `text`/`surface` inversion (a dark background in
light, a light background in dark) — a mid grey would be unreadable in one of the two themes.

<Canvas of={VBadgeStories.Tones} />

## Custom colour

The `color` prop accepts a colour of the consumer's — hexadecimal, a CSS name (`hotpink`) or
`oklch()` — and **replaces the tone**.

> Where `contrast-color()` is not supported (current Chrome/Edge), the text of a custom colour is
> a fixed white: the contrast against a light colour (`gold`, a pastel…) is the consumer's
> responsibility until the function ships there.

<Canvas of={VBadgeStories.CustomColor} />

## Counters

`count` is a **number**; past 99, the display is capped at **"99+"**. The reduced inline padding
keeps the badge round at one or two digits (minimum width = height). The badge carries only a
counter or an icon — for a textual status, prefer a `VChip`.

<Canvas of={VBadgeStories.Counters} />

## Icon

`icon` displays an icon **alone** — an icon name or an explicit render (`{ src }`) — inside a
circular 20px badge (a 16px icon). It wins over `count`.

> The icons the design system renders itself are embedded SVGs: no font is required. Your own
> icon names, on the other hand, come from the font or the library you wire in (see the README).

<Canvas of={VBadgeStories.Icon} />

## Dot

`dot` reduces the badge to a 10px circle with no content — presence, online status, "something
new". Since the information rests on colour alone, provide adjacent text (or an `aria-label` on
an enclosing element) for screen readers.

<Canvas of={VBadgeStories.Dot} />

## Inline

With a target element in the default slot, the badge sits to its right, vertically centred.

<Canvas of={VBadgeStories.Inline} />

## Overlay

`overlay` places the badge on the target's **top-right corner**, slightly tucked inwards (the
logical corner flips in RTL). The offset is a ratio of the badge's own size: it stays
proportionate across the pill, the dot and the icon.

<Canvas of={VBadgeStories.Overlay} />

## Bordered

`bordered` draws a 2px ring in the **colour of the background behind** (`--vectis-color-surface`)
around the badge, to detach it from the element it overlaps. On a different background (a card, a
coloured banner…), overriding `--vectis-color-surface` on the subtree is enough.

<Canvas of={VBadgeStories.Bordered} />

## Accessibility

- The badge is a plain `<span>`: no interactive role wrongly announced.
- The badge carries only a counter or an icon — for a **textual** status, prefer a `VChip` (whose
  role that is).
- In `dot` mode, the information rests on colour alone (invisible to screen readers): provide
  adjacent text or an `aria-label` on the enclosing element.
- The icon (`icon`) is decorative (`aria-hidden`): the meaning must be carried by the context or
  by adjacent text.
