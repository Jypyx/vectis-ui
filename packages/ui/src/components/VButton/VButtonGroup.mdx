# ButtonGroup

Visually joins several `VButton`/`VIconButton` into a **segmented control**: adjacent borders
merged, rounded corners only at the ends of the group.

Since the row is one object, how it is drawn is its own decision, and it is made in two places.
`detached` and `seamless` settle the row itself: whether the buttons are joined at all, and
whether anything is drawn between them once they are. `variant`, `tone`, `size`, `compact`,
`elevated` and `disabled` are set **on the group** and reach every button inside it.

## What the group decides, and what a button keeps

The arbitration is not symmetric, and the asymmetry is the point:

- `variant`, `size`, `compact` and `elevated` are the **shape** of the control, and the group
  wins over the value a button was given. A segment of another height, silhouette or elevation
  no longer lines up with its neighbours, and the merged border stops reading as one object.
- `tone` is **meaning** rather than shape, so the button wins: its own tone first, the group's
  next, `accent` last. That is what lets a single destructive action stand out in the row.
- `disabled` is neither, and is the one that **adds up**: a row switched off cannot have one of
  its segments opt back in, and a segment disabled on its own is not revived by a row that says
  nothing.

A group that names none of them leaves every child exactly as it would have rendered on its own,
which is also why a nested group (a `VToggle`, say) keeps its own rendering inside another one.

## Joined, detached, seamless

Joined is the default: the buttons are pulled onto one another so their borders collapse into a
single line, and a seam is laid over each joint. That seam is a box of its own carrying a 1px
border on one side, which is what keeps its ends square — a neighbour's own border would be
mitred where it meets the transparent top and bottom ones of a filled segment.

`detached` gives up the joining entirely: the buttons are spaced, each keeping its own corners
and its own borders, and an `elevated` row then lets each of them cast its own shadow. Everything
the group hands down still travels.

<Canvas of={VButtonGroupStories.Detached} />

`seamless` keeps the row joined and takes the lines out of it: no seam is generated, and the
borders on **both** sides of every shared edge are cleared, since the segments overlap and a
neighbour's border sits in the same pixel column. The outer edges stay, so an `outline` row reads
as one frame rather than as three boxes. It has no effect under `detached`, where there is no
shared edge to take a line off.

<Canvas of={VButtonGroupStories.Seamless} />

## Full width

`full-width` stretches the row across the whole inline size of its parent, for a segmented
control that stands on a line of its own rather than beside something else. The segments then
take an **equal share** of that width each, whatever their labels measure: a row that reads as
one object cannot be cut into unequal pieces by whichever words happen to be in it.

Nothing of the joining is given up, the segments still overlapping by the pixel the seam is laid
over, and a segment never shrinks below its own label: a row of labels too long for its parent
overflows rather than being crushed into text nothing could then truncate. In a vertical group
it is the width alone, since a column already stretches every segment across it.

## Variants

The seam between segments adapts: for the filled variants (`solid`/`soft`, transparent border) a
thin `--vectis-color-border` line materializes the separation; for `outline` it unifies the joint.
One `variant` on the group covers the whole row.

<Canvas of={VButtonGroupStories.Variants} />

## Elevation

`elevated` raises the row, on the terms of `VButton`'s own prop: the shadow scale on all four
variants, plus the raised surface on `ghost` and `outline`, which have no background of their
own.

The shadow is the ROW's and not each segment's, and that is not a detail: segments overlap by a
pixel, so three shadows would each fall on the neighbour and fill the joints with a dark band
instead of lifting one object off the page. The raised background stays with each button, since
it is what the segment is painted on. Hovering any part of the row therefore raises the whole
of it. Detached, none of that applies and every button casts its own again.

<Canvas of={VButtonGroupStories.Elevated} />

## Vertical orientation

`orientation="vertical"` stacks the buttons: merging and rounded corners switch to the block axis
(top corners of the first, bottom corners of the last). The buttons are equalized in width.

<Canvas of={VButtonGroupStories.Vertical} />

## Icon toolbar

A group of `VIconButton` forms a compact toolbar. Override the default `role` with
`role="toolbar"` (fallthrough on the root) and give the group an `aria-label`. An icon button
reads the group like any other segment, its own `neutral` standing in when the group names no
tone.

## Tooltips, badges and menus on a segment

A segment is not always the button itself. `VTooltip`, `VPopover` and `VBadge` each render a
wrapper around what they are attached to, and the row is drawn through it: the merged borders,
the seam, the corners, the equal shares of a full-width row and the raised focus ring all land
where they would have without one. Bind the `triggerProps` the companion hands out onto the
button, exactly as anywhere else.

`VMenu` puts no wrapper there at all — it renders its trigger and its panel as siblings — so it
combines with any of the three, and the row leaves that panel out of its count: a menu closing a
row keeps the outer corner its trigger is entitled to.

Two things are worth knowing. The row reaches through ONE wrapper, which is what a companion
produces; stacking two of them, a badge inside a tooltip say, is outside the contract. And a
badge on a segment wants `overlay`: an inline badge takes room of its own inside the segment and
pushes the joint away from the button's edge.

## Sizes

The size is a property of the row, so it is named once, on the group: `size` from the scale
shared by every control, and `compact` for the 4px it takes off.

## Accessibility

- The root is a `role="group"` container (overridable to `role="toolbar"` by fallthrough) — give
  it an `aria-label` describing the group (e.g. "Alignment", "Formatting").
- Each `VButton`/`VIconButton` keeps its own semantics and accessible name; a `VIconButton` is
  still required to carry a `label`.
- The focused button rises above its neighbours (`z-index`), so the segment next to it never
  clips its focus ring. It is the only state that rises: a raised segment paints over the pixel
  its neighbour overlaps it by, which is the seam, and on hover that would rub the separation
  out under the pointer.
